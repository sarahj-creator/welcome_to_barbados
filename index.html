Residency & visas

The main routes for living in Barbados, who each one is for, and what it costs. Fees and rules change, so confirm with the official page before you apply.

In detail

Welcome Stamp
For
Remote workers
Fee
US$2,000 / US$3,000
Duration
12 months

A 12-month remote work visa created under the Remote Employment Act 2020, for people employed by, or working for, companies or clients outside Barbados. Employees, freelancers, consultants and business owners can all qualify.

Key requirements

  • Expected annual income of at least US$50,000, shown through an employment letter, bank statements or tax returns.
  • Health insurance covering the full 12 months, either international or bought locally.

Fees and process. US$2,000 for an individual or US$3,000 for a family, non-refundable and due within 28 days of approval. Apply entirely online; an initial response typically comes within 5 to 7 working days. The stamp can be renewed, with a discount on renewal.

Longer term. You may later apply for immigrant status (permanent residency), assessed on your qualifications and experience.

Special Entry & Reside Permit (SERP)
For
Investors, retirees, skills
Fee
US$3,500 / US$5,000
Duration
Indefinite at 60+

Who it's for

  • High-net-worth individuals: net worth over US$5 million, plus US$2 million invested in Barbados or qualifying property.
  • Retired property owners: property worth at least US$300,000, bought with foreign funds and not mortgaged.
  • People with skills considered critical to Barbados' development.
  • Parents or grandparents, aged 60 or over, of Barbadian citizens.

Duration. Apply at 60 or over and the permit is indefinite. Under 60, it's time-limited and renewed until you reach 60.

Fees. BBD $300 to apply, then about US$3,500 (under 60) or US$5,000 (60 and over) on approval. A fee of about US$150 per dependent is also reported.

Health insurance. Required. Sources commonly quote at least US$375,000 of cover under 60 and US$500,000 for 60 and over, but figures vary, so confirm the current amount.

Documents. Form B, birth and marriage certificates, certified passport copy, photos, police certificate of character, proof of funds, proof of property ownership (property route) and proof of health insurance.

Retired property owners can't get a work permit on this route. High-net-worth holders can buy one separately.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Immigration Department
Work permits
For
Job offer in Barbados
Fee
~BBD $300 + $1,500
Duration
Up to 3 years

For people with a confirmed job offer from a Barbados employer. There's no self-sponsored route. Short-term permits last up to 11 months; long-term permits up to 3 years, renewable. Permits are tied to the specific employer and role.

Requirements. For long-term permits, the employer must show no Barbadian could fill the role, for example by advertising locally.

Documents. Passport, photos, a police certificate from every country you've lived in for 6+ months in the past 3 years, a medical form with X-rays, your CV and a detailed employer cover letter.

Processing. Roughly a week once the application is complete, though this varies.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Immigration Department
Returning nationals & family
For
Citizens and families
Key rule
10+ years abroad
Benefit
Duty-free effects

Returning nationals are Barbadian citizens (by birth, descent, registration or naturalisation) coming home after living abroad continuously for 10 years. You'll need proof you lived in Barbados at least 183 days a year before emigrating, and proof of the 10 years abroad, such as tax or social security records, or an affidavit.

Benefits. Duty-free import of household effects and one vehicle. To keep the concession, live in Barbados at least 183 days a year for 3 years in a row and keep the vehicle for 5 years.

Family members. Spouses, children under 18 and dependent parents of Barbadian citizens or residents can apply for dependent residence. Timelines vary, so confirm directly.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Barbados Network Programme
Investment routes

Investors apply through the SERP's high-net-worth category: net worth over US$5 million, plus US$2 million invested in Barbados or qualifying property.

Buying property alone doesn't give you residency, though it supports a SERP application. Barbados has no citizenship-by-investment programme: these routes lead to residence, not a passport.

Take independent legal advice before committing money.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Immigration Department

Cost of living

Barbados is expensive by regional standards, and electricity is the bill that surprises most people. Here's what we can verify, and a calculator to test your own numbers.

2:1Barbados dollars to one US dollar, fixed since 1975
BBD $3.50Flat bus fare, any distance
BBD $4.27Petrol per litre, from 14 September 2026
US$50–150Typical private GP visit

Rent

There's no official rent index. One crowd-sourced estimate puts a central Bridgetown one-bedroom at about BBD $2,367 a month, but it rests on very few entries. West coast parishes cost substantially more. View several places and compare in person.

Groceries

Much of the food is imported, so imported brands cost more. Local markets and fish markets help. No reliable itemised price list exists, so price a typical weekly shop on a scouting trip.

Electricity

Supplied by Barbados Light & Power. The monthly fuel charge is often larger than the base energy charge, and air conditioning pushes bills up fast. Newcomers who aren't citizens or permanent residents pay a security deposit.

Transport

Buses are cheap at a flat BBD $3.50 (about US$1.75). Cars are expensive to buy and insure, so get quotes before you budget for one.

Healthcare

Most newcomers use private GPs and pay for health insurance. See Healthcare & insurance.

Schooling

Public school is free for eligible students. Private and international fees vary widely, so ask each school for its fee schedule. See Schools & families.

Healthcare & insurance

What newcomers need to know about public and private healthcare, insurance, prescriptions and emergency care.

In an emergency

511Ambulance
211Police
311Fire

Main public hospital: Queen Elizabeth Hospital, Martindale's Road, St. Michael. Accident & Emergency: 246-436-6450. Save these numbers to your phone on day one.

How healthcare works here

Barbados has a public system run by the Ministry of Health and Wellness, and a private sector most newcomers use day to day. The Queen Elizabeth Hospital handles emergency and specialist care; polyclinics across the island provide primary care.

Public care is free at the point of use for citizens and permanent residents. Others, including Welcome Stamp and work permit holders, may be charged, so confirm your eligibility with the hospital.

Private facilities commonly used by expats include Bayview Hospital (St. Michael), Sandy Crest Medical Centre (Holetown) and FMH Emergency Medical Clinic (St. Michael).

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Health and Wellness
Registering with a doctor

There's no national registration system. Most newcomers choose a private GP for shorter waits and easier access. A visit typically costs roughly US$50 to US$150, so ask the clinic for its current fee.

Bring your medical history and a list of your medicines, including generic names. A local doctor can then write prescriptions Barbadian pharmacies accept.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Health and Wellness
Health insurance

The Welcome Stamp requires cover for the full 12 months, and the SERP requires cover too. Plans can be international or bought locally.

Complex treatment sometimes means referral abroad, so medical evacuation cover is worth including. Compare inpatient, outpatient, evacuation and pre-existing condition cover. Premiums vary widely by age and plan, so get several quotes.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Barbados Welcome Stamp
Pharmacies & prescriptions

Pharmacies are found across the island, and over-the-counter items such as paracetamol and ibuprofen are freely available. Foreign prescriptions aren't automatically accepted: antibiotics and other regulated medicines need a prescription from a Barbados-licensed doctor.

Bring enough of your regular medicine for the settling-in period, in original labelled packaging, with a letter from your home doctor. Declare it at customs, and check the rules for controlled medicines before you travel.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Health and Wellness
Dental & vision

Public dental care is limited, so most residents go private. As a guide, one Bridgetown practice lists a first examination with four X-rays and an oral cancer screening at BBD $240, with fillings generally BBD $250 to $500.

Optometrists carry out eye exams and prescribe glasses and contact lenses, and some opticians accept vision insurance. Many health plans don't include dental or optical cover, so check before you buy.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Health and Wellness

Schools & families

How the school system works, the international options, and what to arrange before you arrive.

5–16Compulsory school ages
3Terms a year, from September
10–12Age for the secondary entrance exam (BSSEE)
The school system

Public schooling is free and compulsory for ages 5 to 16. Confirm with the Ministry of Education which residency statuses qualify for free places. Non-Barbadian students usually need a student visa to enrol.

International options include The Codrington School in St. John (an IB World School for ages 3 to 18) and Lockerbie College (small classes, ages 8 to 18). Ask each for its current fee schedule.

Terms run roughly September to December, January to April, and April to July; exact dates change yearly. Exams: CXC/CSEC at 16 (similar to GCSEs) and CAPE at 18 (similar to A-Levels).

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Education
Enrolment steps
  1. Shortlist schools.
  2. Contact them to ask about places.
  3. Attend a placement visit or assessment.
  4. Accept a place.
  5. Pay any deposit.

At around ages 10 to 12, students sit the Barbados Secondary School Entrance Examination (BSSEE), which decides secondary placement. Private and international schools set their own application windows, waiting lists and deposits, so contact them early, ideally six months before you move.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Education
Documents you'll need

Typically: birth certificate, passport, previous school reports, immunisation records and proof of address. Non-Barbadian children usually need a student visa.

Ask each school whether records need to be apostilled or translated. It's much easier to arrange before you leave.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Education
School-age groups

Nursery (ages 3 to 4), primary (4 to 11) and secondary (11 to 16, or to 18 for A-Levels). International schools follow their own curriculum stages, so check how year groups map to your home system.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Education
Childcare & early years

Options include daycares, preschools and childminders. Visit in person and ask about staff qualifications, ratios, hours and monthly fees, since there's no reliable published price list.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Education
Family life

Calmer beaches on the west and south coasts, nature parks, sports clubs and swimming lessons. Term and holiday dates change yearly, so check the Ministry of Education calendar when planning trips.

Last verified: September 2026Official source: Ministry of Education

Setting up your life

Banking, driving, utilities, internet, shipping, pets and transport, in roughly the order you'll need them.

Bank accounts

Non-residents can open accounts. Commonly used banks include Republic Bank, CIBC FirstCaribbean, RBC Royal Bank, Scotiabank and First Citizens. Opening deposits generally range from BBD $100 to $500. Ask for the fee schedule before you choose.

You'll need two forms of photo ID, proof of address such as a utility bill, and a reference letter or recent statement from your home bank. Some banks also ask for proof of income.

Get your bank reference letter before you leave. It's much harder to arrange from abroad.

Driving

Barbados drives on the left. Since 15 October 2025, visitors can drive on a valid foreign licence for short stays without a separate permit.

If you're living here (work permit, Welcome Stamp, permanent residency or similar), first get a Trident ID, the national ID card, then exchange your licence at the Barbados Licensing Authority, The Pine, St. Michael. Fees are BBD $50 (1 year), $125 (3 years) or $200 (5 years), cash only.

You'll need your current licence, passport and Trident ID. An International Driving Permit isn't required.

Electricity & water

Electricity. Barbados Light & Power is the only supplier. Your landlord should give you a tenant advice form stating when you become responsible. Apply online, by phone on (246) 626-4300, or at their office. The connection fee is BBD $23.50 for a standard home, and anyone who isn't a citizen or permanent resident pays a security deposit.

Water. Supplied by the Barbados Water Authority. For a new connection, apply in person with photo ID and proof you occupy the property. A deposit of around BBD $350 to $400 has been reported, but from an older document, so confirm the current amount.

Internet & phone

The two main providers are Flow and Digicel, both offering mobile and home broadband. Check coverage at an address before you sign a lease, especially if you work remotely.

Renters usually need a signed landlord permission letter for broadband, and foreign nationals without permanent residency usually pay a security deposit (around $150 has been reported). Prepaid SIMs are sold at the airport, supermarkets and branded stores: bring your passport.

Keep mobile data as a backup if you work remotely.

Shipping belongings

Get several quotes. Sea freight takes roughly 2 to 3 weeks from the US East Coast or 4 to 6 weeks from the UK and Europe, plus 3 to 5 working days for customs clearance once your paperwork is in order.

Duty. Household effects you've owned and used for 12 months or more can generally come in duty-free. Newer items attract import duty (roughly 5% to 60% depending on the item), a 1% environmental levy and VAT. Returning nationals have separate concessions (see Residency & visas).

Moving pets

Barbados removed mandatory quarantine in 2015 for pets that meet the requirements. The steps, in order:

  1. ISO-standard microchip.
  2. Rabies vaccination, given after the microchip.
  3. From low-risk countries: wait 35 days after vaccination. From other countries: a FAVN blood test 30 days after vaccination, then wait 90 days after a successful result.
  4. Apply for an Import Permit from the Veterinary Services Department (valid for 6 months).
  5. Within 7 days of travel: tick and tapeworm treatment (dogs) and an official export health certificate from your vet.

Don't book flights until the paperwork timeline is confirmed.

Getting around

Options are blue Transport Board buses, yellow private buses, white ZR route vans, taxis, or your own car. Bus fares are a flat BBD $3.50; Transport Board buses take exact cash only.

Petrol was BBD $4.27 a litre from 14 September 2026. Cars and insurance are expensive, so get quotes before you budget. If you won't drive, choose a home near a bus route.

Home help & tradespeople

Word of mouth matters most. Ask neighbours, colleagues and community groups for recommendations, agree the price and scope in writing, and avoid paying large sums before work starts.
